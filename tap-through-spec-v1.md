# Verified by NPC — Tap-Through Experience Spec v1
**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting Bayo's review
**Purpose:** What a consumer sees when they tap the orange checkmark on verified content

## The Moment

Someone is watching a TOL clip (or seeing an AI-generated ad, or holding a verified product). They see the orange checkmark. They tap it. What happens next determines whether they trust NPC or bounce.

## Design Principles

1. **Instant.** Verification resolves in under 200ms. No loading spinners, no waiting.
2. **Human-readable.** No hashes, no hex strings, no "blockchain" jargon. Plain language.
3. **Beautiful.** Yeezy first, Apple second. This is a brand moment, not a utility screen.
4. **Why, never how.** The consumer sees what was verified and by whom. Never the methodology.
5. **NPC brand stays minimal.** The checkmark is the hero. "Powered by NPC" is a quiet line at the bottom, not a billboard.

## The Two Checkmarks

- **Orange checkmark** = AI-generated content or AI agent output. NPC orange (#ec662b) is the color of agents and goods.
- **Blue checkmark** = human-generated content. NPC blue (#5e56f6) is the color of humans.

Both carry "Powered by NPC." The color tells the consumer at a glance whether a human or an AI made what they're seeing.

## AI Disclosure Rule

Be explicit. If content was **generated by AI** (synthetic persona, generated ad, AI-written copy), the card says so plainly: "This content was generated by AI."

If content was **clipped, edited, or distributed by AI** but originated from a human (e.g., a TOL clip cut by our clip system from a human-recorded episode), that is different. The card reflects the human origin with the blue checkmark: "Created by [human], clipped by NPC."

The distinction matters because the consumer's question is always: "am I talking to a human or a machine?" We answer it honestly.

## What "Verified" Means

The checkmark means one thing: **NPC verified this is approved, legitimate content — not nefarious.** We verified the origin, the authorization, and the claims. The consumer can trust it isn't a scam, an impersonation, or a manipulation.

We do not stamp scams. The verification process is the filter.

## The Flow

### Step 1: The Tap
User taps the orange checkmark on any verified surface (clip, ad, product page, physical tag scan).

### Step 2: The Verification Card (appears instantly)
A bottom-sheet card slides up with:

- **Large orange checkmark** — the trust signal, unmistakable
- **"Verified by NPC"** — the brand, clear
- **What was verified** (plain language, one line):
  - For a clip: "This clip is from Thinking Out Loud EP4, published Oct 9, 2026"
  - For an AI ad: "This ad was generated by [Brand]'s verified AI agent"
  - For a product: "This is an authentic [Product Name] from [Creator]"
- **Verified on** — timestamp
- **One action:** "Learn more" (expands) or "Verify something else" (input field)

### Step 3: Expanded Details (if they tap "Learn more")
- Who verified it (the agent or entity)
- What claims were checked (authenticity, origin, authorization)
- A simple timeline: created → verified → viewed

### Step 4: The Bridge
At the bottom of the card, a quiet line:
- "Powered by NPC Labs — the trust layer for commerce"
- Tapping goes to npclabs.xyz

This is where the ad for a product becomes an ad for NPC. Not pushy, just present.

## Technical Requirements

- Verification API already exists (v1, 90 tests passing)
- Need: consumer-facing resolution endpoint (credential ID → human-readable verification card data)
- Need: embeddable checkmark component (web, iOS, Android)
- Need: bottom-sheet UI component (matches TOL/NPC design system)
- Target: <200ms from tap to card render

## Phases

**Phase 1 (now):** Static verification cards on TOL clips. Each clip gets a verification page URL. Tapping the checkmark opens the URL. Proves the UX loop.

**Phase 2:** Dynamic resolution via API. Any verified credential resolves to a live card.

**Phase 3:** Embedded components for third-party use. Brands integrate the checkmark into their own surfaces.

## Open Questions — Resolved by Bayo (2026-10-08)

1. **NPC brand prominence:** Minimal. The checkmark is the hero; "Powered by NPC" is a quiet line.
2. **AI disclosure:** Explicit. AI-generated content is labeled as such. AI-clipped/distributed human content keeps the blue checkmark with human origin noted.
3. **Failed verification:** A failed verification means we stamped something we shouldn't have — content that looks like a scam or has opaque, unverifiable history. The system must catch this before the stamp, not after. If a verification is revoked, the checkmark is pulled and the card shows: "This verification has been revoked."
