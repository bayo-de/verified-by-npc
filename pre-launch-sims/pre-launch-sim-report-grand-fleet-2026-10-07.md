## Sentiment trajectory
Bold open → proof-vs-claims sorts the room (one real artifact moves Dev-D; liability silence hardens Client-C) → settles at founder-curious with enterprise blocked.

## Ranked objections
1. **Liability.** "When your agent makes a $10k mistake, who pays?" No answer in the material — blocks "for hire" entirely. (Client-C) — HIGHEST
2. **"Just a guy with Claude."** The burden of proof is on demos, not claims. (Dev-D, Hype-skeptic)
3. **Dispute resolution between agents.** Who adjudicates? Honest answer exists (in progress) but isn't packaged. (Jordi-type)
4. **Branding over substance.** "Grand Fleet" reads as cosplay to outsiders. (Hype-skeptic, Competitor)
5. **Verification theater.** The root-of-trust question follows every trust-layer launch. (All skeptics)

## Failure modes
- **Liability silence kills enterprise deals** before they start — the first serious prospect asks, and there's nothing.
- **Demo-or-die:** claims without live demos read as hype; one pilot isn't a portfolio.
- **The name oversells** to anyone outside the community; insiders love it, outsiders smirk.

## Decision: FIX (Bayo to sign)
1. **Write the liability answer before any "for hire" conversation.** Even "in progress with counsel, here's the interim position" beats silence. This is the gate.
2. **Lead with the Inbox Watch pilot, not the metaphor.** Package it as the demo: problem, 3 verification rounds, 18 findings, live pipeline untouched. Proof first, navy second.
3. **Keep "Grand Fleet" internal.** Externally: "verified agents" + proof. Let the work carry the poetry, not the name.
